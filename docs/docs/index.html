Research summary

Abstract

This project asks whether historical search-performance signals can help prioritize which published content pages deserve human review for possible refresh work.

Using the FlyRank ML Internship dataset, I analyzed 85,967 eligible pages and evaluated models on a grouped client holdout of 4,247 pages.

I compared a transparent staleness-and-visibility baseline with Logistic Regression and Random Forest using five pre-decision search features.

Logistic Regression produced the strongest fresh result, reaching 55% precision@20 compared with 20% for the rule baseline, while overall ROC-AUC remained modest at 0.5537.

The final output is a human-review action queue designed to prioritize editorial investigation rather than automate content changes.

01 · Problem

Introduction / Problem statement

The project asks whether historical search-performance signals can help prioritize which published content pages deserve human review for possible refresh work.

The unit of analysis is a published content page. The analysis produces model scores and a ranked queue that helps an editor decide which pages to inspect first.

The decision

The system does not decide what should be rewritten. It helps a human reviewer decide where limited editorial attention should go first.

A wrong high-priority call can waste editorial effort or encourage an unnecessary change. A wrong low-priority call can delay review of a page that may deserve attention.

Machine learning is useful here because the portfolio contains far more content-performance observations than a person can review manually.

03 · Method

Methodology

The task is treated as a ranking problem around a binary future_decline proxy.

A page is labeled positive when its average daily impressions during March 22–31, 2026 are less than 80% of its average daily impressions during March 1–21, 2026.

Compared approaches

  1. Transparent rule baseline — staleness + visibility
  2. Logistic Regression — simple learned reference
  3. Random Forest — non-linear comparison and Week 7 playbook scorer

Validation design

Validation uses a grouped holdout by client_hash_id with random_state=42. Pages belonging to the same client therefore cannot appear in both training and test data.

Training clients

Leakage check

The feature and outcome windows are separated, future-window values and label-derived fields are excluded, and pseudonymous IDs are used only for grouping rather than prediction.

04 · Results

Results vs baseline

The held-out positive base rate is 41.96%. Precision@K is therefore interpreted relative to that context rather than as a standalone number.

Method Precision@10 Precision@20 ROC-AUC
Rule baseline 10% 20% 0.4760
Logistic Regression 40% 55% 0.5537
Random Forest 40% 50% 0.5529

Takeaway: both learned models improved top-of-queue precision over the transparent rule baseline, but overall discrimination remained modest.

Headline result

Logistic Regression produced the strongest fresh result: 55% precision@20 versus 20% for the transparent rule baseline.

Logistic Regression matched Random Forest at precision@10, exceeded it at precision@20, and had a slightly higher ROC-AUC.

The learned models therefore provide useful ranking signal at the top of the queue, but their ROC-AUC values around 0.55 show that the five historical features do not fully explain future decline.

05 · Interpretation

What the models actually found

Earlier permutation-importance analysis suggested that gsc_observed_days, past_clicks, and past_ctr carried more useful signal than the other features.

past_impressions contributed less, while past_avg_position did not clearly improve generalization on held-out clients.

Two recurring error patterns

First, pages with weak historical engagement can receive high decline-risk scores even when later performance remains stable. Weak CTR or clicks should therefore not automatically trigger a refresh.

Second, some pages with healthy-looking historical impressions and clicks still decline later. Static historical totals cannot capture every temporal, seasonal, or content-specific factor.

Interpretation

The finding is directional rather than definitive: historical search signals can improve prioritization over a simple baseline, but they are not strong enough to support automatic content decisions.

06 · Limits

Limitations & honest framing

This analysis is observational and predictive. It does not show that refreshing a page causes an improvement in search performance.

  • future_decline is an operational proxy, not a complete definition of content deterioration.
  • The held-out evaluation contains only eight clients, so results may differ for substantially different client or content distributions.
  • The population excludes pages without sufficient observed GSC coverage or at least 100 historical impressions.
  • Historical impressions, clicks, CTR, position, and observed-day coverage do not capture every factor affecting search performance.
  • Overall ROC-AUC remains close to 0.55, indicating modest discrimination.
What this paper does not claim

A high model score does not prove that a page needs a refresh and does not imply that a refresh will improve future performance.

Human-review rule

Before changing content, a reviewer should inspect freshness, seasonality, search intent, content overlap, SERP context, and business value.

Do not automate

The score should not automatically rewrite or publish content, delete pages, change redirects or canonicals, modify metadata, or make revenue and spending decisions.
